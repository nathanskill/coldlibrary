Canon: A shadow does not move. The explanation lies in its being made anew.

Explanation: Shadow. Where the light arrives, the shadow is gone. If the shadow seems to stay, it is because it is ended and renewed without cease.

Canon: Two shadows. The explanation lies in doubling.

Explanation: Two shadows: two lights hold the object between them. One light, one shadow. The shadows answer to the lights.

Canon: The image is inverted. Where the rays cross there is a point, and the image lengthens from it. The explanation lies in the point.

Explanation: Image. The light comes to the person and shines through like an arrow. What is low enters and goes high; what is high enters and goes low. The feet screen off the light from below, so their image is formed above; the head screens off the light from above, so its image is formed below. Whether the object is far or near, there is a point through which the light passes, and so the image is formed within the screened chamber.

Canon: A shadow facing the sun. The explanation lies in the light turning back.

Explanation: Shadow. When the light of the sun is turned back to shine on a person, the shadow falls between the sun and the person.

Canon: The size of a shadow. The explanation lies in slanting or upright, far or near.

Explanation: Shadow. When the post slants, its shadow is short and large; when the post stands upright, its shadow is long and small. If the light is smaller than the post, the shadow is larger than the post. It is not only a question of small and large, but of far and near.

Canon: Standing before a mirror, the image is turned about; there are many, yet they seem few. The explanation lies in the smallness of the area.

Explanation: Before a level mirror the image is single. Its appearance, white or black, far or near, slanting or upright, follows the light. The image and the person who looks belong together: when one approaches, the other approaches; when one withdraws, the other withdraws; each faces the other, back to back. Whatever the person who looks presents, the mirror reflects it all. The images of what is reflected are countless, but each must pass through the true line. Because they pass through the true line, the image is turned about. So it is the same wherever the body stands.

Canon: In a hollow mirror there are two images, one small and inverted, one large and upright. The explanation lies in being outside or inside the centre.

Explanation: Mirror. The divided mirror: inside the centre, when the object is near the centre, what is reflected is large and the image is large; when it is far from the centre, what is reflected is small and the image is small, but it is always upright. It begins from the centre and follows the true line, lengthening it. Outside the centre, when the object is near the centre, what is reflected is large and the image is large; when it is far from the centre, what is reflected is small and the image is small, but it is always inverted. It meets at the centre and follows the true line, lengthening it.

Canon: In a rounded mirror there is one image, diminished and always upright. The explanation lies in what it takes in.

Explanation: Mirror. When the object is near, what is reflected is large and the image is large; when it is far, what is reflected is small and the image is small, but it is always upright.
