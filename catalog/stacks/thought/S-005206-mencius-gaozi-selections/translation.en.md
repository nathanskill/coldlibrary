## 8

Mencius said: “The trees of Ox Mountain were beautiful once. But because it stood at the edge of a great city, axes and hatchets cut them down: could it stay beautiful? Even so, with the rest it had by day and by night and the moisture of rain and dew, shoots and sprouts did come up; but then cattle and sheep were brought to graze, and that is why it is as bare as you see it. People see the bareness and suppose it never had timber. But is that the nature of the mountain? And in a person, too, how could there be no heart of humaneness and rightness? The way a man loses his good heart is like axes and hatchets against trees. Cut down morning after morning, can it stay beautiful? With the rest it has by day and by night, in the air of the early dawn, his likes and dislikes come a little close to other people’s; but then what he does in the day fetters it and destroys it. Fettered over and over again, the night air is not enough to keep it alive. When the night air is not enough to keep it alive, he is not far from the birds and beasts. People see that he is like a bird or a beast, and suppose he never had the capacity. But is that the true condition of a man? So, given its nourishment, there is nothing that will not grow; deprived of its nourishment, there is nothing that will not wither. Confucius said: ‘Hold on to it and it stays; let it go and it is lost. It comes and goes at no fixed time, and no one knows where it lives.’ Was he not speaking of the heart?”

## 9

Mencius said: “Do not wonder that the king is not wise. Take the easiest-growing thing in the world: give it one day of sun and ten days of cold, and it will never grow. I see him only seldom, and as soon as I withdraw, the ones who chill him arrive; what can I do for the few shoots there are? Now chess is a small art; yet if you do not give it your whole mind and will, you will not master it. Yi Qiu was the best player in the whole state. Suppose Yi Qiu teaches two men to play. One gives it his whole mind and will and listens only to Yi Qiu. The other listens too, but his whole mind is on a swan that may be coming over, and how he might draw his bow and shoot a corded arrow at it. Though he learns beside the first, he will not be his equal. Is it because his intelligence is not equal? I say: it is not.”

## 11

Mencius said: “Humaneness is the human heart; rightness is the human road. To leave the road and not walk it, to let go of the heart and not know how to look for it: how sad that is. When people’s chickens or dogs stray, they know to go after them; when the heart strays, they do not know to go after it. The way of learning is nothing else than to go after the heart that has strayed.”

## 13

Mencius said: “A tung or a catalpa a hand’s span or two thick: anyone who wants it to grow knows how to nourish it. But their own person they do not know how to nourish. Do they love their own person less than a tung or a catalpa? It is that they do not think, and badly.”

## 15

Gongduzi asked: “We are all alike human. Yet some become great men and some become small men. Why?”

Mencius said: “Those who follow the greater part of themselves become great men; those who follow the lesser part become small men.”

“We are all alike human. Yet some follow the greater part and some the lesser. Why?”

“The organs of the ear and the eye do not think, and they are clouded by things. When a thing meets a thing, it only draws it along. The organ of the heart thinks. If it thinks, it gets it; if it does not think, it does not get it. This is what Heaven has given us. If first one sets up the greater part, the lesser cannot take it away. That is all it is to be a great man.”

## 19

Mencius said: “The five grains are the finest of seeds; but if they do not ripen, they are worth less than wild millet and barnyard grass. Humaneness, too, is simply a matter of bringing it to ripeness.”

## 20

Mencius said: “When Yi taught archery, he always set his mind on the full draw of the bow, and those who learn must also set their minds on the full draw. When a master carpenter teaches, he always uses the compass and the square, and those who learn must also use the compass and the square.”
