## Book 1 · Learning

The Master said: "To learn something and, in due time, to practise it: is that not a pleasure? To have friends come from far away: is that not a joy? To go unrecognised by others and feel no resentment: is that not the mark of a gentleman?"

Master You said: "Few who are good sons and good younger brothers like to defy their superiors, and there has never been one who disliked defying his superiors yet liked to stir up disorder. The gentleman works at the root. When the root is set, the Way grows from it. Being a good son and a good younger brother: is this not the root of humaneness?"

The Master said: "Clever words and a pleasing face: seldom is there humaneness in them."

Master Zeng said: "Every day I examine myself on three points. In acting on behalf of others, have I been less than loyal? In dealing with friends, have I failed to keep my word? Have I passed on what I have not practised myself?"

The Master said: "To guide a state of a thousand chariots: be attentive in affairs and keep faith, be sparing in expenditure and love the people, and employ the people only at the proper seasons."

The Master said: "A young person should be a good son at home and respectful to elders outside, careful and trustworthy, loving towards all, and close to those who are humane. If there is strength left over after this, let it be used to study letters."

Zixia said: "Someone who values worth above looks, who in serving his parents can give all his strength, who in serving his lord can give his whole person, and who keeps his word with his friends: though he may say he has not studied, I would certainly call him learned."

The Master said: "A gentleman who is not serious will not be respected, and his learning will not be solid. Hold loyalty and good faith as first principles. Have no friends who are not your equal. When you make a mistake, do not be afraid to correct it."

Master Zeng said: "Take care over the end of life and remember those long gone, and the people's virtue will grow deep again."

Ziqin asked Zigong: "Whenever the Master arrives in a state, he always comes to hear about its government. Does he seek this out, or is it offered to him?" Zigong said: "The Master gets it by being cordial, good, respectful, frugal and deferential. The Master's way of seeking it is surely different from the way other people seek it."

The Master said: "While a man's father is alive, observe his intentions; when his father is gone, observe his conduct. If for three years he does not change his father's ways, he may be called a good son."

Master You said: "In the practice of ritual, harmony is what is most valued. In the ways of the former kings, this was the beautiful thing, and they followed it in matters great and small. But there are times when it does not work: if you know harmony and seek harmony without regulating it by ritual, that will not work either."

Master You said: "If your promises are close to what is right, your word can be kept. If your respect is close to ritual, you will keep shame and disgrace at a distance. If you rely on those you should not neglect, they too can be your mainstay."

The Master said: "A gentleman who does not seek to eat his fill or to live in comfort, who is quick in action and careful in speech, and who goes to those who have the Way to be set straight: he may be called fond of learning."

Zigong said: "Poor but not servile, rich but not arrogant: how is that?" The Master said: "It will do. But it is not as good as poor yet joyful, rich yet loving ritual." Zigong said: "The Book of Songs says, 'As if cut, as if filed, as if carved, as if polished.' Is this what it means?" The Master said: "Ci, now I can begin to talk about the Songs with you. Tell you what has gone before, and you know what is to come."

The Master said: "Do not worry that others do not know you. Worry that you do not know others."

## Book 2 · Government

The Master said: "To govern by virtue is like the pole star: it stays in its place, and all the other stars turn around it."

The Master said: "The three hundred Songs can be summed up in one phrase: no swerving in thought."

The Master said: "Guide the people with orders and keep them in line with punishments, and they will stay out of trouble but have no sense of shame. Guide them with virtue and keep them in line with ritual, and they will have a sense of shame and set themselves right."

The Master said: "At fifteen I set my heart on learning. At thirty I stood firm. At forty I had no doubts. At fifty I knew the will of heaven. At sixty my ear was attuned. At seventy I could follow what my heart desired without overstepping the line."

Meng Yizi asked about being a good son. The Master said: "Do not disobey." When Fan Chi was driving his chariot, the Master told him: "Meng asked me about being a good son, and I answered, 'Do not disobey.'" Fan Chi said: "What did you mean?" The Master said: "While they are alive, serve them according to ritual. When they die, bury them according to ritual, and make offerings to them according to ritual."

Meng Wubo asked about being a good son. The Master said: "Give your father and mother no cause to worry about anything but your health."

Ziyou asked about being a good son. The Master said: "Nowadays being a good son means being able to feed one's parents. But even dogs and horses are fed. Without respect, where is the difference?"

Zixia asked about being a good son. The Master said: "The hard part is the expression on one's face. When there is work to be done, the young take on the labour; when there is wine and food, the elders are served first. Is that all it takes to be a good son?"

The Master said: "I talk with Hui all day long, and he never disagrees, as if he were slow. But when he has withdrawn and I look at his private life, I find he has drawn out what I said. Hui is not slow at all."

The Master said: "See what a person does. Look at how he does it. Observe what he is at peace with. How can anyone stay hidden? How can anyone stay hidden?"

The Master said: "Someone who keeps the old warm and so comes to know the new can be a teacher."

The Master said: "A gentleman is not a vessel made for one use."

Zigong asked about the gentleman. The Master said: "He first puts his words into practice, and only then lets his words follow."

The Master said: "The gentleman is inclusive and not partisan. The small man is partisan and not inclusive."

The Master said: "To learn without thinking is to be lost. To think without learning is to be in danger."

The Master said: "To attack a different doctrine only does harm."

The Master said: "You, shall I teach you what knowing is? When you know something, to recognise that you know it; when you do not know something, to recognise that you do not know it. That is knowing."

Zizhang was studying in order to obtain an official post. The Master said: "Hear much, leave aside what is doubtful, and speak carefully about the rest, and you will make few mistakes. See much, leave aside what is risky, and act carefully on the rest, and you will have few regrets. Few mistakes in speech, few regrets in action: the post will follow from that."

Duke Ai asked: "What should I do so that the people will be won over?" Confucius answered: "Raise up the straight and set them over the crooked, and the people will be won over. Raise up the crooked and set them over the straight, and the people will not be won over."

Ji Kangzi asked: "How can the people be made respectful, loyal and eager?" The Master said: "Approach them with dignity, and they will be respectful. Be a good son and kind to all, and they will be loyal. Raise up the good and teach those who are not able, and they will be eager."

Someone said to Confucius: "Why do you not take part in government?" The Master said: "The Book of Documents says, 'Be a good son, only a good son, and a friend to your brothers.' To carry this into the affairs of a household is also to take part in government. Why must one hold office to take part in government?"

The Master said: "A person without good faith: I do not know how he can manage. A large cart without a yoke-bar, a small cart without a collar-bar: how can they be made to go?"

Zizhang asked: "Can we know what things will be like ten generations from now?" The Master said: "The Yin took over the rites of the Xia, and what they removed and added can be known. The Zhou took over the rites of the Yin, and what they removed and added can be known. Whoever may succeed the Zhou, even a hundred generations from now, can be known."

The Master said: "To make offerings to spirits that are not your own is to flatter. To see what is right and not do it is to lack courage."

## Book 4 · Dwelling in Humaneness

The Master said: "It is humaneness that makes a neighbourhood beautiful. How can someone be called wise who, having the choice, does not settle among the humane?"

The Master said: "Those who are not humane cannot endure hardship for long, nor enjoy happiness for long. The humane are at peace in humaneness; the wise make use of it."

The Master said: "Only the humane can truly like people, and truly dislike them."

The Master said: "If you set your heart on humaneness, you will do no wrong."

The Master said: "Wealth and rank are what people want, but if they cannot be had in the right way, do not keep them. Poverty and low station are what people hate, but if they cannot be escaped in the right way, do not run from them. If a gentleman abandons humaneness, how can he make a name? A gentleman does not depart from humaneness even for the space of a meal. In haste and hurry he holds to it; in stumbling and falling he holds to it."

The Master said: "I have never seen anyone who truly loved humaneness, or anyone who truly hated what is not humane. One who loves humaneness puts nothing above it. One who hates what is not humane is practising humaneness, because he will not let what is not humane touch him. Is there anyone who can devote all his strength to humaneness for a single day? I have never seen anyone whose strength was not enough. Perhaps there is such a person, but I have never seen him."

The Master said: "People's faults are true to their kind. Look at someone's faults, and you will know their humaneness."

The Master said: "If I hear the Way in the morning, I can die content in the evening."

The Master said: "A scholar who has set his heart on the Way but is ashamed of poor clothes and poor food is not worth talking to."

The Master said: "The gentleman, in his dealings with the world, is not set for or against anything. He sides with what is right."

The Master said: "The gentleman cares about virtue; the small man cares about his land. The gentleman cares about the law; the small man cares about favours."

The Master said: "To act always with an eye to profit is to invite much resentment."

The Master said: "If a state can be governed with ritual and deference, what difficulty is there? If a state cannot be governed with ritual and deference, what good is ritual?"

The Master said: "Do not worry that you have no position; worry about how to be fit for one. Do not worry that no one knows you; seek to be worth knowing."

The Master said: "Shen, my Way has one thread running through it." Master Zeng said: "Yes." When the Master had gone out, the disciples asked: "What did he mean?" Master Zeng said: "The Master's Way is loyalty and reciprocity, and nothing more."

The Master said: "The gentleman understands what is right; the small man understands what is profitable."

The Master said: "When you see someone worthy, think of becoming their equal. When you see someone unworthy, look inward and examine yourself."

The Master said: "In serving your father and mother, remonstrate gently. If you see they are not inclined to follow you, remain respectful and do not oppose them; take on the burden and do not complain."

The Master said: "While your father and mother are alive, do not travel far. If you must travel, let them know where you are going."

The Master said: "If for three years he does not change his father's ways, he may be called a good son."

The Master said: "You should always know the age of your father and mother: on the one hand with joy, on the other with concern."

The Master said: "The ancients were slow to speak, because they would be ashamed if their actions did not keep up with their words."

The Master said: "Those who go wrong through holding back are few."

The Master said: "The gentleman wants to be slow in speech and quick in action."

The Master said: "Virtue is never alone. It always has neighbours."

Ziyou said: "In serving a lord, if you are too insistent you will be disgraced. With friends, if you are too insistent you will be kept at a distance."

## Book 7 · Transmitting

The Master said: "I transmit and do not invent. I trust in and love the ancient ways. In this I venture to compare myself with our old Peng."

The Master said: "To take things in quietly, to learn without tiring, to teach others without growing weary: which of these can I claim?"

The Master said: "Virtue not cultivated, learning not discussed, hearing what is right and not being able to move towards it, having faults and not being able to correct them: these are my worries."

In his leisure hours the Master was relaxed and easy, cheerful and at ease.

The Master said: "How far I have declined. It is a long time since I dreamed of the Duke of Zhou."

The Master said: "Set your heart on the Way, base yourself on virtue, lean on humaneness, and take your recreation in the arts."

The Master said: "From anyone who brought even a bundle of dried meat as a gift, I have never withheld instruction."

The Master said: "I do not open the way for those who are not eager, nor help out those who are not struggling to speak. If I show someone one corner and he does not come back with the other three, I do not repeat the lesson."

When the Master ate beside someone in mourning, he never ate his fill. On a day when he had wept, he did not sing.

The Master said to Yan Yuan: "To act when employed and to stay hidden when set aside: only you and I can do this." Zilu said: "If you were leading the three armies, whom would you take with you?" The Master said: "Not someone who would wrestle a tiger with his bare hands or wade across a river and die without regret. It would have to be someone who approaches each task with caution and likes to plan things through to success."

The Master said: "If wealth could be properly sought, I would seek it, even as a man with a whip clearing the road. If it cannot be properly sought, I will follow what I love."

The things about which the Master was careful: fasting, war, and illness.

When the Master was in Qi he heard the Shao music, and for three months he did not notice the taste of meat. He said: "I never imagined that music could reach such heights."

Ran You said: "Is the Master on the side of the ruler of Wei?" Zigong said: "Well, I shall ask him." He went in and said: "What sort of men were Boyi and Shuqi?" The Master said: "Worthy men of old." "Did they have regrets?" "They sought humaneness and found it. What would they regret?" Zigong came out and said: "The Master is not on his side."

The Master said: "Coarse rice to eat, water to drink, and my bent arm for a pillow: there is joy in this too. Wealth and rank gained by wrong means are to me like drifting clouds."

The Master said: "Give me a few more years, so that I may study the Changes at fifty, and I may be free of great faults."

The Master used the standard pronunciation for the Songs, the Documents and the performance of ritual. In all of these he used the standard pronunciation.

The Duke of She asked Zilu about Confucius, and Zilu did not answer. The Master said: "Why did you not say: 'He is the kind of man who forgets to eat when he is eager, forgets his worries in his joy, and does not notice old age coming on'?"

The Master said: "I was not born knowing. I love the ancient ways and have worked hard to seek them out."

The Master did not talk about prodigies, feats of strength, disorder, or spirits.

The Master said: "When three people walk together, there is sure to be a teacher for me among them. I pick out what is good in them and follow it, and what is not good I correct in myself."

The Master said: "Heaven gave birth to the virtue in me. What can Huan Tui do to me?"

The Master said: "My friends, do you think I am hiding something from you? I hide nothing from you. There is nothing I do that I do not share with you. That is who I am."

The Master taught four things: letters, conduct, loyalty and good faith.

The Master said: "A sage I shall never get to see; I would be content to see a gentleman." The Master said: "A truly good person I shall never get to see; I would be content to see someone who is constant. Pretending to have what one lacks, to be full when one is empty, to be at ease when one is in want: it is hard to be constant like that."

The Master fished with a line but not with a net. When he shot with a corded arrow, he did not shoot at birds roosting.

The Master said: "There may be those who make things up without knowing, but I am not one of them. To hear much, choose what is good and follow it; to see much and remember it: this is the next best thing to knowing."

The people of Huxiang were hard to talk to. When a boy from there came to see the Master, the disciples were puzzled. The Master said: "I approve of his coming forward, not of his going back. Why be so severe? When someone has cleansed himself to come forward, I approve of his being clean; I do not vouch for his past."

The Master said: "Is humaneness far away? If I want humaneness, there it is."

The Minister of Justice of Chen asked: "Did Duke Zhao know ritual?" Confucius replied: "He knew ritual." After Confucius had withdrawn, the minister bowed to Wuma Qi, invited him forward, and said: "I have heard that a gentleman is not partial. Is a gentleman partial after all? The duke took a wife from Wu, of the same clan name, and called her Wu Mengzi. If he knew ritual, who does not know ritual?" Wuma Qi reported this. The Master said: "I am fortunate. If I make a mistake, others are sure to notice it."

When the Master was singing with others and someone sang well, he would ask for the song again, and then join in.

The Master said: "In letters I am perhaps as good as others. But as for living as a gentleman in my own conduct, that I have not yet achieved."

The Master said: "How could I dare to claim sagehood or humaneness? But to work at them without tiring and to teach others without growing weary: that much can be said of me." Gongxi Hua said: "That is exactly what we disciples cannot learn."

The Master was gravely ill, and Zilu asked to offer prayers. The Master said: "Is there such a thing?" Zilu replied: "There is. The prayer says: 'We pray to you, spirits above and below.'" The Master said: "My praying has gone on for a long time."

The Master said: "Extravagance leads to presumption, frugality to shabbiness. Better shabby than presumptuous."

The Master said: "The gentleman is calm and at ease. The small man is always anxious."

The Master was warm yet stern, dignified yet not fierce, respectful yet at ease.

## Book 9 · The Master Seldom

The Master seldom spoke of profit, or of fate, or of humaneness.

A man from the village of Daxiang said: "How great Confucius is. He has learned so widely, yet he has made no name in any one thing." When the Master heard of this, he said to his disciples: "What shall I take up? Driving? Archery? I shall take up driving."

The Master said: "A cap of hemp is what ritual prescribes; nowadays silk is used, which is more frugal, and I follow the majority. To bow at the foot of the steps is what ritual prescribes; nowadays people bow at the top, which is presumptuous. Though it goes against the majority, I bow at the foot."

The Master was free of four things: no guessing, no insisting, no obstinacy, no self.

When the Master was in danger in Kuang, he said: "Since King Wen is gone, is not the culture here, with me? If heaven had meant to destroy this culture, those who came after would never have been given a share in it. Since heaven has not destroyed this culture, what can the people of Kuang do to me?"

The Grand Steward asked Zigong: "Is your Master a sage? How many things he can do." Zigong said: "Heaven has surely set him on the way to sagehood, and he can do many things besides." When the Master heard this, he said: "Does the Grand Steward know me? When I was young I was of humble station, so I learned to do many ordinary things. Does a gentleman need to be able to do many things? No, not many."

Lao said: "The Master said, 'I was never given an office, so I learned the arts.'"

The Master said: "Do I have knowledge? No, I do not. If a simple man asks me something, I come to it quite empty. I tap at both ends of the question and go through it to the bottom."

The Master said: "The phoenix does not come, the river gives out no chart. It is all over with me."

When the Master saw someone in mourning dress, or someone in ceremonial cap and robes, or someone blind, he would rise at once, even if they were younger than he was; and if he passed them, he would quicken his step.

Yan Yuan sighed and said: "The more I look up at it, the higher it seems; the more I bore into it, the harder it becomes. I see it in front of me, and suddenly it is behind. Step by step the Master leads people on so well. He broadens me with letters and restrains me with ritual. Even if I wanted to stop, I could not. I have used up all my ability, and something seems to stand there, tall before me. Though I want to follow it, I can find no way."

The Master was gravely ill, and Zilu had the disciples act as his retainers. When the illness eased a little, the Master said: "How long Zilu has been carrying on this pretence. I have no retainers, yet he acts as if I had. Whom am I deceiving? Am I deceiving heaven? Besides, would I not rather die in the arms of you, my friends, than in the arms of retainers? And even if I am not given a grand funeral, am I going to die by the roadside?"

Zigong said: "Suppose there is a beautiful piece of jade here. Should one wrap it up and store it away in a box, or seek a good price and sell it?" The Master said: "Sell it, sell it. I am waiting for the right price."

The Master wanted to go and live among the nine tribes of the east. Someone said: "They are so rough. What would you do?" The Master said: "If a gentleman lived among them, what roughness would there be?"

The Master said: "Only after I came back from Wei to Lu was the music put right, and the Ya and the Song each found their proper place."

The Master said: "To serve the high officials when abroad and to serve my father and elder brothers at home; not to dare to be less than diligent in matters of mourning; not to be overcome by wine: which of these can I claim?"

The Master stood by a river and said: "It flows on like this, never stopping, day or night."

The Master said: "I have never seen anyone who loves virtue as much as he loves beauty."

The Master said: "It is like building a mound: if I stop when only one basket of earth is needed, it is I who stopped. It is like levelling ground: if I go on, even if I have tipped only one basket, it is I who went forward."

The Master said: "The one who never grew slack when I talked to him: that was Hui, was it not?"

The Master said of Yan Yuan: "What a loss. I saw him go forward; I never saw him stop."

The Master said: "There are shoots that never flower. There are flowers that never bear fruit."

The Master said: "The young are to be held in awe. How do we know that those to come will not be equal to us now? But if someone reaches forty or fifty and nothing has been heard of him, then he is not worth holding in awe."

The Master said: "Can one help agreeing with words of correct counsel? But what counts is to change oneself. Can one help being pleased with words of gentle advice? But what counts is to think them through. If someone is pleased but does not think it through, agrees but does not change, there is nothing I can do for him."

The Master said: "Hold loyalty and good faith as first principles. Have no friends who are not your equal. When you make a mistake, do not be afraid to correct it."

The Master said: "The three armies can be robbed of their commander, but a common man cannot be robbed of his will."

The Master said: "Wearing a tattered padded robe, standing beside those in fox and badger furs, and not feeling ashamed: that would be Zilu, would it not? 'Not envious, not covetous: how could he be anything but good?'" Zilu recited these lines all the time. The Master said: "That is the Way, but is it enough to be good?"

The Master said: "Only when the year turns cold do we see that the pine and cypress are the last to fade."

The Master said: "The wise are not perplexed, the humane are not anxious, the brave are not afraid."

The Master said: "There are those with whom one can study, but not yet travel the Way. There are those with whom one can travel the Way, but not yet take a stand. There are those with whom one can take a stand, but not yet weigh things as circumstances change."

"The flowers of the cherry tree, how they flutter and turn. It is not that I do not think of you, but your house is so far away." The Master said: "He did not really think of her. If he had, what distance would there be?"
