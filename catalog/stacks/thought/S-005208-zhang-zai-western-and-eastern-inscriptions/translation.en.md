## The Western Inscription

Heaven is called my father and Earth my mother. Even a speck like me has a place, at one with them, in their midst. So what fills Heaven and Earth I take as my body, and what commands Heaven and Earth I take as my nature. All people are my brothers and sisters; all things are my companions. The great ruler is my parents’ eldest son, and his great ministers are the stewards of the eldest son’s household. To honour the aged is to treat them as the elders of the family; to be kind to the orphaned and the weak is to treat them as its young. The sage is one whose virtue is joined with theirs; the worthy are the finest among them. All under Heaven who are weary and infirm, crippled and sick, without brothers or children, widowed or without a wife, are my own brothers in distress who have no one to turn to. To protect them at such times is the care a son gives; to rejoice and not to fret is filial piety at its purest. To go against this is called betraying virtue; to harm humaneness is called being a thief. One who helps evil along has no worth; one who lives up to the form he was given is the one who truly resembles his parents. One who understands their transformations carries on their work well; one who fathoms their spirit carries on their purpose well. To have nothing to be ashamed of even in the most hidden corner of the house is to bring them no disgrace; to keep the heart and nourish the nature is never to grow slack. The son of the lord of Chong cared for his parents by refusing fine wine; to bring up the gifted is to share one’s goodness with one’s kind, as the border warden of Ying did. To labour without rest until his father was content was the merit of Shun; to have nowhere to flee and wait to be boiled was the reverence of Shensheng. To give back whole what he had received was Shen; to be bold in following and obey the command was Boqi. Wealth and rank, blessing and plenty, will make my life rich; poverty and low station, sorrow and care, are there to polish you like jade into what you are to become. While I live, I follow and serve; when I am gone, I am at rest.

## The Eastern Inscription

Playful words come out of thought; playful acts are made by intention. They issue in the voice and show in the four limbs, and to say they were not from one’s own heart is to be unclear; to want others not to suspect one is impossible. Mistaken words are not from the heart; mistaken acts are not sincere. One slips in speech and blunders with the four limbs, and to say this was what one meant is to deceive oneself; to want others to follow one is to deceive them. Some lay what came from the heart to their own play, and call what came from a lapse of thought their own sincerity. They do not know to guard against what does come out of them, and they blame what did not come out of them; so they grow proud and persist in their faults. What ignorance could be greater?
