My heart is unusually lonely.

Yet my heart is very calm: without love or hate, without joy or grief, without colour or sound.

I am probably growing old. My hair is already grey; is that not plain? My hands tremble; is that not plain? Then the hands of my soul must tremble too, and its hair must be grey.

But that was many years ago.

Before that, my heart was once full of bloody songs: blood and iron, flame and poison, recovery and revenge. Then all at once these became empty, though sometimes I deliberately filled the emptiness with a helpless, self-deceiving hope. Hope, hope: I took this shield of hope to hold off the dark night that attacks out of the emptiness, though behind the shield there was still the dark night in the emptiness. And so, little by little, I used up my youth.

Did I not know long ago that my youth had gone? But I thought the youth outside me still remained: the stars, the moonlight, the stiff fallen butterflies, the flowers in the dark, the ill-omened words of the owl, the cuckoo weeping blood, the vagueness of laughter, the dance of love ... Though it was a sad and uncertain youth, it was youth all the same.

But why is it so lonely now? Can it be that even the youth outside me has gone, and that the young people of the world have grown old too?

There is nothing for it but to grapple with this dark night in the emptiness myself. I have put down the shield of hope, and I hear the song "Hope" by Petőfi Sándor (1823–49):

What is hope? A harlot:

She lures everyone and gives herself to all;

When you have sacrificed your greatest treasure,

Your youth, she casts you off.

It is seventy-five years since this great lyric poet, this patriot of Hungary, died for his country on the points of Cossack spears. Death is sad, but sadder still is that his poems have not died even now.

But how pitiful is life. Even Petőfi, so proud and brave, halted at last before the dark night, and looked back towards the boundless East. He said:

Despair is as vain as hope.

If I must still drag out my life in this "vanity" that is neither light nor dark, I will still seek that lost, sad and uncertain youth, even if it lies outside me. For if the youth outside me is extinguished, the twilight within me will wither too.

But now there are no stars and no moonlight, no stiff fallen butterflies, no vagueness of laughter, no dance of love. Yet the young people are very calm.

There is nothing for it but to grapple with this dark night in the emptiness myself. Even if I cannot find the youth outside me, I must at least cast away myself the twilight within me. But where is the dark night? Now there are no stars, no moonlight, no vagueness of laughter, no dance of love; the young people are very calm, and before me there is not even a real dark night.

Despair is as vain as hope.

(1 January 1925)
