The two mountains Taihang and Wangwu were seven hundred miles around and ten thousand fathoms high. They stood south of Jizhou and north of Heyang. The Foolish Old Man of the North Mountain was nearly ninety years old, and his house faced the mountains. He was troubled that they blocked the way to the north, so that every journey in and out was long and roundabout. He gathered his household and said to them: "Let us all put our strength together and level these heights, so that a road runs straight to the south of Yu and reaches the southern bank of the Han. Shall we?" They all agreed at once. His wife raised a doubt: "With your strength you could not even wear down the little hill of Kuifu. What can you do to Taihang and Wangwu? And where will you put the earth and stones?" They all said: "We will throw them into the end of the Bohai Sea, north of Yintu." So he led those of his sons and grandsons who could carry a pole, three men in all, to break up the rocks and dig the soil and carry it in baskets and hods to the end of the Bohai Sea. Their neighbour, the widow of the Jingcheng family, had a son just old enough to be losing his milk teeth, and he skipped along to help them. Winter turned to summer before they made one trip there and back. The Wise Old Man of the River Bend laughed and tried to stop him, saying: "How foolish you are. With the strength you have left in your last years, you cannot even damage a single hair of the mountain. What can you do with all that earth and stone?" The Foolish Old Man of the North Mountain sighed deeply and said: "Your mind is so stubborn that nothing can get through it. You are not even as sensible as the widow and her little boy. Even when I die, my sons will remain. My sons will have grandsons, and the grandsons will have sons; those sons will have sons, and those sons will have grandsons. Sons and grandsons, generation after generation, will never come to an end. But the mountains will not grow any higher. Why should we worry that they cannot be levelled?" The Wise Old Man of the River Bend had no answer. The god who holds the snakes heard of this and was afraid that the old man would never stop, so he reported it to the Lord of Heaven. The Lord of Heaven was moved by his sincerity and ordered the two sons of Kua'e to carry the mountains away on their backs, one to be set down east of Shuo and the other south of Yong. From that time on, from the south of Ji to the southern bank of the Han, there has been no high ground in the way.
