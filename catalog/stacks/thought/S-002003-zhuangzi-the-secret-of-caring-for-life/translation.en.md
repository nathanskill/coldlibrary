My life has a limit, but knowing has no limit. To use what has a limit to chase what has none is dangerous. To go on pursuing knowledge after seeing this is more dangerous still. If you do good, stay clear of fame; if you do wrong, stay clear of punishment. Follow the middle course as your constant guide, and you can keep your body whole, keep your life intact, care for your parents, and live out your years.

Cook Ding was carving an ox for Lord Wenhui. Wherever his hand touched, wherever his shoulder leaned, wherever his foot stepped, wherever his knee pressed, there was a whisper, a rush, and the blade sang as it went in, never out of tune: in step with the dance of the Mulberry Grove, in time with the music of the Jingshou. Lord Wenhui said: "Ah, wonderful. Can skill really reach such heights?" Cook Ding laid down his knife and answered: "What your servant cares about is the Way, which goes beyond skill. When I first began carving oxen, all I could see was the ox. After three years, I no longer saw the whole ox. Now I meet it with my spirit and do not look with my eyes. The senses know when to stop, and the spirit moves where it will. I follow the natural grain, strike into the great hollows, guide the blade through the large openings, and go by what is already there. I never touch a ligament or tendon, let alone the big bones. A good cook changes his knife once a year, because he cuts. An ordinary cook changes his knife once a month, because he hacks. My knife has been in use for nineteen years and has carved several thousand oxen, yet its edge is as fresh as if it had just come from the whetstone. There are spaces between the joints, and the edge of the blade has no thickness. When what has no thickness enters where there is space, there is plenty of room, more than enough for the blade to play in. That is why after nineteen years my knife is as fresh as if it had just come from the whetstone. Even so, whenever I come to a knotted place and see that it will be difficult, I take care and go warily. My gaze settles, my movements slow, I move the blade very gently, and with a soft sound it comes apart, like a clod of earth crumbling to the ground. I stand with the knife in my hand and look all around, and linger, content; then I wipe the knife and put it away." Lord Wenhui said: "Wonderful. I have listened to the words of Cook Ding and learned how to nourish life."

When Gongwen Xuan saw the Commander of the Right, he was startled and said: "What sort of man is this? Why does he have only one foot? Was it heaven, or was it man?" He said: "It was heaven, not man. When heaven gave this man life, it made him one-footed. A man's form is something given. By this I know it was heaven and not man." The pheasant of the marshes takes ten steps for one peck and a hundred steps for one drink, but it does not long to be kept in a cage. There its spirit might be in fine condition, but it would not be happy.

When Lao Dan died, Qin Shi went to mourn him, gave three cries, and left. A disciple said: "Were you not the master's friend?" "I was." "Then is it right to mourn him like this?" "It is. At first I took him for a true man, but now I see he was not. When I went in to mourn just now, there were old people weeping for him as if for a son, and young people weeping for him as if for a mother. For them to have gathered like that, there must have been some who spoke without wanting to speak and wept without wanting to weep. That is to flee from heaven and turn against the truth of things, forgetting what we have received. The ancients called it the punishment for fleeing heaven. The master came because it was his time; the master went because it was his turn. Be at peace with the time and settle into the turn, and neither grief nor joy can get in. The ancients called this being set free by the Lord of Heaven." What feeds the flame is spent as fuel, but the fire passes on, and no one knows where it ends.
