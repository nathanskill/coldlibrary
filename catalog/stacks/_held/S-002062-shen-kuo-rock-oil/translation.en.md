In the districts of Fu and Yan there is rock oil. The old saying that "Gaonu county produces a fatty water" refers to this. It comes up at the water's edge, mixed with sand, stones and spring water, seeping out slowly. The local people soak it up with pheasant tail feathers and collect it in jars. It looks very like pure lacquer and burns like hemp, but its smoke is very thick, and any tent or curtain it touches turns black. I suspected the smoke might be useful, so I tried sweeping up its soot to make ink. The ink was black and glossy like lacquer, better than pine-soot ink, and so I made a great deal of it. The ink marked "Rock Liquid of Yanchuan" is this. This substance will surely come into wide use in later times, and it began with me. For rock oil is very plentiful and is produced endlessly within the earth, unlike pine trees, which in time are used up. Today the pine forests of Qi and Lu are gone, and gradually those of the Taihang, the west of the capital and the south of the Yangzi are going too; most of the pine-covered hills are now bare. Those who make soot have perhaps not yet learned the advantages of rock smoke. The smoke of coal is also heavy, and blackens people's clothes. I once wrote a playful poem on Yanzhou: "Below Erlang Mountain the snow falls thick and fast; we pitch felt tents and live like the people of the frontier. Our white clothes all turn black before the winter is out; the rock smoke is as thick as the dust of Luoyang."
