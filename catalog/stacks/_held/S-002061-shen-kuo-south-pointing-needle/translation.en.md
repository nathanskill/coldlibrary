Diviners rub the point of a needle with a lodestone, and then it can point south; but it always inclines slightly to the east and does not point due south. Floated on water, it tends to waver and swing. It can also be balanced on a fingernail or on the rim of a bowl, where it turns more quickly, but these surfaces are hard and slippery and it easily falls off. Hanging it from a thread is best. The method is to take a single strand of silk from a fresh cocoon and fix it to the middle of the needle with a speck of wax the size of a mustard seed. Hang it where there is no wind, and the needle will always point south. Among such needles there are some that point north after rubbing. In my household we have some that point south and some that point north. That the lodestone points south is like the cypress pointing west: no one can find the reason for it.
