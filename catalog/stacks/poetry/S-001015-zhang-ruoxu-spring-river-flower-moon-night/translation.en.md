The spring river's tide runs level with the sea,
and over the sea the bright moon rises with the tide.
Shimmering, it follows the waves ten thousand li:
where on the spring river is there no moonlight?

The river winds around the fragrant meadows;
the moon on the flowering woods looks like sleet.
Frost seems to drift in the air, and you cannot see it fall;
the white sand on the shoals is lost to sight.

River and sky are one colour, without a speck of dust;
bright, bright in the empty sky, the moon's lone wheel.
Who on this riverbank first saw the moon?
In what year did the river moon first shine on a person?

Generation after generation, human lives go on without end;
year after year, the river moon looks just the same.
I do not know whom the river moon is waiting for;
I see only the long river sending its water on.

A single white cloud drifts slowly away;
on Green Maple Bank the sorrow is more than one can bear.
Whose boat is out tonight, whose wanderer?
Where is the moonlit tower of the one who longs for him?

Pity the moon above that tower, lingering:
it must be shining on her mirror stand.
Roll up the curtain at her jade door, and the light will not go;
on the washing-stone she brushes it off, and it comes back.

At this hour we gaze at the same moon and cannot hear each other;
I wish I could follow the moonlight and flow down to shine on you.
The wild geese fly far, but they cannot carry the light;
fish and dragons leap in the depths, and the water writes in ripples.

Last night by the quiet pool I dreamed of falling flowers:
half of spring is gone, and you are not home.
The river water carries the spring away until it is almost gone;
over the river pool the setting moon slants west again.

The slanting moon sinks deep, hidden in the sea mist;
from Jieshi to the Xiao and Xiang, the roads are endless.
Who knows how many are going home by this moonlight?
The setting moon stirs the heart and fills the river trees.
