Softly I am leaving,
as softly as I came;
softly I wave my hand
in farewell to the clouds of the western sky.

The golden willows by the river
are brides in the setting sun;
their bright reflections in the shimmering water
ripple in my heart.

The green floating-hearts that grow from the soft mud
sway sleekly under the water:
in the gentle waves of the Cam
I would gladly be a water weed.

The pool beneath the elms
is not a clear spring but a rainbow from the sky,
crumpled among the floating weeds,
where a rainbow-coloured dream has settled.

To seek a dream? Pole a long punt
upstream to where the green grass is greener,
with a boatload of starlight
sing aloud in the dazzle of the stars.

But I cannot sing aloud:
quietness is the music of parting;
even the summer insects are silent for me,
and silent tonight is Cambridge.

Quietly I am leaving,
as quietly as I came;
I wave my sleeve
and take away not a single cloud.

6 November, on the China Sea.
