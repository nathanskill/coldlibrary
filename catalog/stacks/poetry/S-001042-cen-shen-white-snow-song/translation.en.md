The north wind rolls over the land and snaps the white grass;
under the frontier sky, in the eighth month, the snow is already flying.
As if overnight a spring wind had come,
and on a thousand trees, ten thousand trees, the pear blossom opened.
It drifts through the pearl blinds and wets the silk curtains;
fox furs are not warm, brocade quilts are thin.
The general's horn bow cannot be drawn;
the protector-general's iron armour is too cold to put on.
Across the great desert the ice lies a hundred fathoms deep;
gloomy clouds hang frozen for ten thousand li.
In the commander's tent we set out wine for the one going home,
with western fiddles, pipa and Qiang flutes.
Flurry on flurry, the evening snow falls on the camp gate;
the wind tugs at the red flag, frozen too stiff to flap.
At the east gate of Luntai I see you off;
as you go, the snow covers the road through the Tian Shan.
The mountain bends, the road turns, and I cannot see you;
on the snow there is only the empty place where your horse went.
