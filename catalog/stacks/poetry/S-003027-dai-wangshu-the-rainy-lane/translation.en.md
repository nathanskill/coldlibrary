Holding an oil-paper umbrella, alone
I wander down a long, long
and lonely rainy lane,
hoping to meet
a girl like a lilac,
knotted with sorrow.

She has
the colour of the lilac,
the fragrance of the lilac,
the sadness of the lilac,
grieving in the rain,
grieving and wandering;

she wanders down this lonely rainy lane,
holding an oil-paper umbrella
just like me,
just like me
pacing in silence,
cold, desolate, and sad.

Silently she comes near,
comes near, and casts
a glance like a sigh;
she drifts past
like a dream,
like a dream, forlorn and lost,

as in a dream there drifts
a spray of lilac,
this girl drifts past me;
silently she goes further, further,
to the crumbling fence,
to the end of this rainy lane.

In the sad song of the rain
her colour fades,
her fragrance scatters,
fades and scatters, even her
glance like a sigh,
her lilac sadness.

Holding an oil-paper umbrella, alone
I wander down a long, long
and lonely rainy lane,
hoping there will drift past me
a girl like a lilac,
knotted with sorrow.
