When young I had no ear for the common tune;
my nature loved the hills and mountains.
By mistake I fell into the dusty net
and was gone for thirty years.
The caged bird longs for its old woods,
the pond fish thinks of its old deep pool.
I have cleared waste ground at the edge of the southern wilds;
keeping to my simple ways, I have come back to garden and field.
Ten mu or so of land around the house,
a thatched cottage of eight or nine rooms.
Elms and willows shade the back eaves,
peach and plum stand in rows before the hall.
Dim, dim, the distant village;
soft, soft, the smoke above the houses.
A dog barks deep in the lane,
a cock crows at the top of a mulberry tree.
No dust or clutter at my gate and yard;
in the empty rooms there is leisure to spare.
Long I was kept inside a cage,
and now I have come back to what is natural.
