## I
Yu Xin's writing grew finer as he aged:
his brush soared to the clouds, his thought ranged free.
People today scoff and pick at the poems he left,
as if the master of the past should fear those who came after.

## II
Yang, Wang, Lu and Luo wrote in the manner of their day;
the lightweights mock their writing without end.
You and your names will both perish,
but the rivers will not stop flowing for ten thousand ages.

## III
Even granting that Lu and Wang, when they took up the brush,
fell short of Han and Wei, who stood near the Odes and the Sao,
they were dragon-marked, tiger-backed horses fit for a king to drive;
watch them cross a city in a stride, and then look at yourselves.

## IV
In talent you can hardly outdo those few masters;
of all the poets now, who stands out from the crowd?
At most you see kingfishers perched on orchid stems,
not one who can haul a whale out of the blue sea.

## V
I do not slight the moderns, and I love the ancients;
clear words and fine lines are always my neighbours.
If we hope to climb to Qu Yuan and Song Yu, we should ride beside them;
otherwise I fear we will only follow in the dust of Qi and Liang.

## VI
That you fall short of the worthies before you, there is no doubt;
each learns from the one before, so who comes first?
Sort out the false styles and keep close to the Odes;
to learn from teacher after teacher: that is your teacher.
