Chuzhou is ringed by mountains. The peaks to the southwest have the most beautiful forests and valleys. The one that looks so lush, deep and graceful is Langya. Walk six or seven miles into the mountains and gradually you hear the murmur of water; the stream that pours out between two peaks is the Yielding Spring. As the peaks turn and the path bends, there is a pavilion perched above the spring like a bird with its wings spread: this is the Old Drunkard's Pavilion. Who built the pavilion? A monk of the mountain called Zhixian. Who named it? The governor, after himself. The governor comes here with his guests to drink. After only a little wine he is drunk, and he is also the oldest of them, so he calls himself the Old Drunkard. The Old Drunkard's heart is not in the wine but in the mountains and the water. The joy of mountains and water is found in the heart and lodged in the wine.

When the sun rises and the mist in the woods lifts, and when the clouds return and the caves in the cliffs grow dark, this change from light to dark is morning and evening in the mountains. Wild flowers open and give off a hidden scent; fine trees grow tall and cast a deep shade; the wind is high and the frost is clean; the water falls and the stones appear: these are the four seasons in the mountains. Going in the morning and coming back in the evening, the scenery of the four seasons is never the same, and so the joy too is endless.

As for people carrying loads singing on the road, travellers resting under the trees, those in front calling and those behind answering, the bent old and the children led by the hand, coming and going without end: these are the people of Chuzhou out walking. Fishing by the stream, where the water is deep and the fish are fat; making wine from the spring, where the spring is fragrant and the wine is clear; wild game and mountain greens set out in a pleasant jumble: this is the governor's feast. The pleasure of the feast is not in strings or flutes. Someone hits the target with an arrow, someone wins at go, the drinking cups and counting sticks are mixed together, people stand up and sit down in noisy chatter: this is the guests enjoying themselves. And the one with the weathered face and white hair, slumped among them: that is the governor, drunk.

Before long the setting sun is on the mountain, and people's shadows are scattered. The governor goes home and the guests follow him. The woods grow dark and shaded, and birdsong rises and falls: the visitors have gone and the birds are happy. But the birds know the joy of the mountains and forests, and do not know the joy of people. The people know the joy of going out with the governor, and do not know that the governor's joy is in their joy. The one who can share their joy when drunk and write it down when sober is the governor. And who is the governor? Ouyang Xiu of Luling.
