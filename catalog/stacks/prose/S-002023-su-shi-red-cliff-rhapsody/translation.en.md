In the autumn of the year renxu, on the sixteenth night of the seventh month, Master Su and his guests went boating below the Red Cliff. A cool breeze blew gently, and the water lay without a ripple. Raising our cups to one another, we recited the poem of the bright moon and sang the verse about the lovely maiden. After a while the moon rose above the eastern hills and lingered between the Dipper and the Ox. A white mist lay across the river, and the gleam of the water reached up to the sky. We let our little reed of a boat go where it would, out over ten thousand acres of shimmering emptiness. Vast and free, as if riding the air and driving the wind, not knowing where we would stop; light and drifting, as if leaving the world behind and standing alone, growing wings and rising to join the immortals.

Then we drank and were very happy, and beat time on the side of the boat and sang. The song went: "Oars of cassia, sweeps of orchid, striking the clear water, moving upstream through the flowing light. Far, far away is what I long for: my fair one, at the other side of the sky." One of the guests played the flute, following the song in harmony. Its sound was low and mournful, as if longing, as if yearning, as if weeping, as if pleading. The lingering notes trailed on, thin as a thread and never broken, enough to make the dragons hidden in the dark gorges dance and the widow in a lonely boat weep.

Master Su's face grew sad. He straightened his robe, sat up and asked his guest: "Why does it sound like that?"

The guest said: "'The moon is bright, the stars are few, the crows and magpies fly south': is that not a poem of Cao Mengde? Looking west towards Xiakou and east towards Wuchang, where mountains and river twist together in dense green: is this not where Mengde was hemmed in by young Zhou Yu? When he had taken Jingzhou and come down to Jiangling, and was sailing east with the current, his ships stretched stem to stern for a thousand miles, his banners hid the sky. He poured wine as he faced the river and composed poems with his spear across his knees. He was surely the hero of his age. Yet where is he now? And you and I, fishermen and woodcutters on the river islands, with fish and shrimp for companions and deer for friends, riding a boat like a single leaf and raising gourd cups to each other: we are mayflies lodged between heaven and earth, a single grain of millet in the vast sea. I grieve that our life is so brief, and envy the Long River that has no end. I would like to take the flying immortals by the arm and roam with them, to hold the bright moon and last forever. I know this cannot be had all at once, so I have given these lingering notes to the sad wind."

Master Su said: "Do you know the water and the moon? The water flows away like this, yet it has never gone. The moon waxes and wanes like that, yet in the end it neither grows nor shrinks. If you look at it from the side of change, heaven and earth do not last for the blink of an eye. If you look at it from the side of what does not change, then things and we ourselves are both without end. So what is there to envy? Besides, everything between heaven and earth has its owner. If something is not mine, I will not take even a hair of it. Only the cool breeze on the river and the bright moon over the mountains, which the ear takes in as sound and the eye meets as colour: no one forbids us to take them, and using them never wears them out. They are the inexhaustible treasury of the maker of things, and you and I can enjoy them together."

The guest was pleased and laughed. We washed the cups and poured again. When the food and fruit were finished, the cups and plates lay scattered about, and we fell asleep against one another in the boat, not knowing that the east was already growing light.

I wrote this rhapsody last year and have not lightly shown it to anyone; only one or two people have seen it. Qinzhi sent a messenger asking for something I had written recently, so I copied it out myself to send to him. These are troubled times and I am wary of trouble. Qinzhi cares for me and will surely keep it hidden and not let it out. There is also a later Red Cliff rhapsody, but my brush is tired and I have not been able to write it out; it will have to wait for the next letter. Shi.
