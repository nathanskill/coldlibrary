A mountain need not be high: if an immortal lives there, it is famous. Water need not be deep: if a dragon lives there, it is sacred. This is a humble room, but my virtue gives it fragrance. Moss climbs green up the steps; the green of the grass comes in through the blinds. Those who talk and laugh here are great scholars; no one unlettered comes and goes. Here I can tune a plain zither and read the golden sutras. No strings or flutes to trouble the ear, no official papers to weary the body. Zhuge Liang's thatched hut in Nanyang, Ziyun's pavilion in western Shu. As Confucius said: "What humbleness would there be?"
