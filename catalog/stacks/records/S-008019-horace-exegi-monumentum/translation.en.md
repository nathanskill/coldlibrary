I have finished a monument more lasting than bronze,
loftier than the royal pile of the pyramids,
one that no gnawing rain, no wild north wind
can tear down, nor the numberless
procession of the years, nor the flight of time.
I shall not wholly die; a great part of me
will slip past the goddess of the grave. I shall go on
growing, always new, in the praise of those to come,
as long as the high priest climbs the Capitol
with the silent Vestal at his side.
I shall be spoken of where the wild Aufidus roars,
and where Daunus, poor in water, once ruled
a people of the fields: that I, grown strong from humble roots,
was the first to bring the song of Aeolia
into Italian measures. Take up the pride
your merits have earned, Melpomene,
and be glad to crown my hair with Delphic laurel.
