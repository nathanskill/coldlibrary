I have finished a monument more lasting than bronze,
loftier than the royal pile of the pyramids,
one that no gnawing rain, no wild north wind
can tear down, nor the numberless
procession of the years, nor the flight of time.
I shall not wholly die; a great part of me
will slip past the goddess of the grave. On and on
I shall grow, fresh in the praise of those to come, while up the Capitol
the high priest climbs beside the silent Vestal.
I shall be spoken of where the wild Aufidus roars,
and where Daunus, poor in water, ruled a people
of the fields: that I, grown strong from humble roots,
was the first to bring the song of Aeolia into Italian
measures. Take up the pride
your merits have earned, Melpomene, and gladly
crown my hair with Delphic laurel.
