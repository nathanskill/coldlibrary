## Chaucer's Words to Adam, His Own Scribe

Adam, my scribe, if ever it falls to you
to write out Boethius or Troilus anew,
may you get the scab under your locks
unless you copy truer to what I made.
So often in a day I must redo your work,
correct it, and rub and scrape it too;
and all of it through your carelessness and haste.

## Troilus and Criseyde, Book V, lines 1786–1799

Go, little book, go, my little tragedy,
where God may send your maker yet, before he dies,
the power to make some comedy!
But, little book, envy no other work,
and be the servant of all poetry;
and kiss the steps wherever you see pass
Virgil, Ovid, Homer, Lucan and Statius.

And since there is so great a diversity
in English and in the writing of our tongue,
I pray to God that no one miscopy you,
or spoil your metre for want of the right speech.
And wherever you are read, or else sung,
I beg of God that you be understood!
But now, back to what I was saying before.
